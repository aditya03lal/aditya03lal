● INDIAN ECONOMY

• History
— Pre Liberalization: Focus on public sector development (steel, machinery), Nationalization of core sectors (banking, coal).
— Average growth of 3.5 % (While Singapore and Hong Kong boasted 7 %, overtaking even the UK in GDP per capita).
— 1991 Liberalization Triggers: Rajiv Gandhi assassination, USSR collapse, foreign exchange crisis, IMF pressure.
— Under PV Narasimha Rao and Manmohan Singh (Chaotic but transformative, we had no figure like Thatcher, Reagan or Xiaoping).
— Stabilization period + Setbacks (97-03): industrial decline, droughts, recessions, 6-7 % average growth since then.

• Current Performance
— Q2 FY26: GDP jumped 8.2 %, defying global slowdown. Strong growth in manufacturing, services, and investment.
— Growth averaging 7.85 % over past 2 years, debunks "deflator washout hypothesis" (that low inflation is artificially inflating growth).
— ICOR (Incremental Capital-Output Ratio) improving. 8.2 % growth with only 34 % investment (ICOR of 5 would require 40 %).
— Increase in productivity + Stable prices with no inflation i.e. This growth is real and sustainable.
— Trade deficit doubled (Good sign: Sharp increase in capital goods imports).

— Services: IT, Finance, Digital Economy (55%), Industry/Manufacturing: Automobiles, Pharma, Construction (27%), Agriculture (18%).
— Growth Drivers: GST and tax reforms reducing logistical frictions + Public Capex (Roads, Ports, Railways) + Fiscal Discipline.
— Foreign Investment: 1,600+ MNCs opening Global GCCs in design, R&D, and AI (creating spillover effects), Enabling 100+ unicorns.
— Defence production, space, and nuclear power opened to private players (Though renewable energy and storage advancing faster + cheaper).
— Nuclear concerns: High capital costs, Overruns (See France), Committing to massive targets (100 GW by 2047), Waste Management, Public Opposition.

• Union Budget 26/27
— Record spending on infrastructure: 7 high-speed rail corridors planned + 20 national waterways development.
— Foreign Investments: Loosened Equity Investment Limits (PROI) + Sector-Specific Tax Holidays and Exemptions + Regulatory Simplifications (CIS-cargo).
— Tax holiday for foreign-owned data centers (Till 2047), 5-year income tax exemption for non-residents supplying capital goods.
— Domestic: ₹ 10K cr. for MSME Champions Scheme + ₹10K cr. for BioPharma Shakti Scheme (Biologics/Pharma) + SHE Marts for women entrepreneurs.
— NRIs: Tax Collected at Source (TCS) reduced from 5% to 2% (Overseas remittances), 6-month foreign asset disclosure scheme to avoid prosecution.

● FOREIGN TRADE

• India-Europe FTA
— Preferential access to 99.5 % of Indian exports (zero duty for softgoods, jewelry). India to drop tariffs on automobiles, alcohol, premium foods.
— EU pledge $100 billion FDI over next 15 years + Creation of 1 million jobs, Frameworks supporting student, professional, and business mobility.
— India makes commitments on labor, gender, and environmental standards for sustainable development. Concerns ⌄⌄
— EU influencing domestic laws and economic regulations, including emerging sectors like AI. (Pharmaceutical industry: FTA reinforces voluntary licensing).
— EU carbon tax to cuts emissions by just 0.1% but impacts Indian agricultural exports. Zero duty on green goods may constrain domestic growth.

• India-USA Interim Trade Deal
— India drops tariffs on strategic industries: agricultural machinery, healthcare equipment, AI hardware. Tariffs reduced for alcohol and tree nuts.
— Lower duty on fresh fruits and DDGs, in limited quantities. India still protecting staple agricultural and dairy products, meat, ethanol.
— US drops tariffs from 50% to 18% on softgoods (0% on select foods and spices, pharmaceuticals, precious stones). 
— Contingent on India bringing US imports to $500 B over five years, including crude and petroleum products, pivoting away from Russia.

● TRUMP 2.0

• History
— US shifted from isolationism to global leadership post-WW2, viewing Soviet Union as existential threat (Was already the largest economy by 1890).
— Bretton Woods (1944): Established dollar as global reserve currency, created IMF and World Bank to facilitate global trade and financial stability.
— Altruistic Hegemony: US-led order created global prosperity through open markets, democratic values, and multilateral institutions (WTO, Paris Climate Agreement).
— Trust in the Dollar: 58% of global reserves in dollars (89% of forex is in $), 54% of trade in dollars (Nixon abandoned the gold standard in 1971).
— China/UK attempts at creating a global currency have failed (Shanghai bubble - Yuan crisis in 2015; UK capital flight).

— Trump shift: Hawkish foreign policy eroding global goodwill + Bypassing democratic processes to fast-track agenda.
— Fed rate cuts fueled stock markets (4.3% GDP growth, yet unemployment 4.0 > 4.6%). Big Investments in AI infra (capital-intensive but creates few jobs).
— Tariff costs passed to consumers may double (1/3 > 2/3). Fed may halt rate cuts to curb inflation, risking Republican defeat in the Congressional elections.

• Tariff Policy
— History: US mfg offshored to Japan post WW2 (70s-80s: cars, electronics, consumer goods), high quality at lower cost, efficient/lean manufacturing (Toyota). 
— China (80s-now: electronics, consumer goods) with Xiaoping's economic reforms + NAFTA (1994) and WTO entry (2001) became the global manufacturing hub.
— US lost a quarter of manufacturing jobs in the industrial Midwest (dubbed 'Rust Belt'). Trump in his economic nationalism blames hyperglobalization.
— Objectives: Reverse trade deficits + Negotiate with China + Protect strategic industries (EVs, Semicon, Solar, Steel and Alu.) + Political appeal for MAGA.
— Effects: Slowdown in global growth (IMF says 3.3% to 3.1%), Currency devaluations, Trade Rerouting. US - Increase IN costs of essentials, electronics, housing.

• Why this is the wrong fight / Way forward
— Dean Baker (Researcher): "The tariff formula is like a doctor dividing your birthday by your height".
— Germany: Trade deficit reversed yet factory jobs declined at rates similar to Ohio (Cause: Automation and global supply chains).
— Trade deficits reflect foreign capital inflows supporting domestic investment and fiscal expansion + Global manufacturing's GDP share has stagnated post-2009. 
— Focus on reshoring critical industries, economic diversification, workforce development, raising global wages (low wages drive global imbalances).
— Target trade imbalances that drive economic tension and instability + Work towards a fluid world order.

• Russia-Ukraine

— Precursor: Crimea annexation and Donbas war (2014). Current: Russia occupies approximately 20% of Ukrainian territory.
— Russian Concerns: NATO expansion eastward is seen as a threat, Russia wants to maintain frontier/buffer states.
— Ukraine's strategic importance: Natural resources including rare earth elements, gas reserves, uranium, and iron ore (among the largest deposits in Europe).
— Pushback: Long-range warfare, $400B+ international aid, diplomatic resistance (sanctions, peace negotiations involving the US).

• USSR's Fall (1991)

— Bureaucratic Dysfunction: Excessive centralization, corruption, red tape, and inefficiency plagued the political system.
— Economic Collapse: Lack of innovation, shortage in commodities, centrally controlled demand and supply.
— Republics (like Kazakhstan) demanded autonomy due to resource exploitation and Moscow's centralized control.
— Socioeconomically strong (free housing, healthcare, education), but couldn't compensate for economic dysfunction.

# BRICS

**Overview**
- Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa
- Expanded 2024: Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, UAE added
- Represents major emerging economies and Global South

**Core Objectives**
- Reduce dependence on Western-dominated financial systems (de-dollarization)
- New Development Bank (2014) as alternative to IMF/World Bank
- Push for multipolar world order and reform of global institutions
- Greater representation for developing nations in global governance

**BRICS Chair 2026: India**
- Annual rotating chairmanship
- Expected priorities: digital infrastructure, BRICS payment systems, counter-terrorism, reform of multilateral institutions
- India's balancing act: managing China tensions while cooperating, maintaining strategic autonomy (Quad vs BRICS)
- Key challenge: managing diverse interests among expanded membership with competing geopolitical priorities
